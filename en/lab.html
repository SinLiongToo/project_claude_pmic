What to look for

  • In classic mode press "Ceramic": phase margin falls below 20° at heavy load, while light load is stable. That is why old LDOs oscillate with ceramic capacitors.
  • Press "Tantalum": the ESR zero restores phase. Now push ESR above 5 Ω: gain lifts at high frequency and it is unstable again. The map shows a "tunnel" with failures on both sides.
  • Switch to internal compensation with ceramic: stable. Drop COUT to 0.22 µF: crossover moves up to the parasitic poles and it oscillates — the origin of the minimum-capacitance spec.

Parameters LDO after a buck

Pass-device RDS(on) 0.3 Ω; loop gain starts to fall when headroom is below dropout + 0.25 V. At high frequency PSRR is set by the divider between coupling capacitance and COUT (with 0.6 nH ESL).

What to look for

  • Low-frequency PSRR is set by loop gain and starts falling near the loop bandwidth; at high frequency the capacitor divider holds it up until ESL resonance, after which it worsens.
  • Cut headroom below 0.15 V: the pass device enters the linear region, PSRR drops 20–40 dB and buck ripple passes straight through.
  • Watch the buck-frequency line: 70 dB at 1 kHz does not mean 70 dB at 2 MHz.
  • Switch to NMOS: the source follower isolates the input itself and the whole curve moves up.

Parameters load step

Pass current follows the error voltage through a two-pole loop and is slew-rate limited; finite loop gain leaves a steady load-regulation error.

Output voltage deviation ΔVOUT

Currents load vs pass device

What to look for

  • The vertical jump at the step is ΔI·ESR; the slope after it is the capacitor supplying the load alone before the loop reacts: ΔI·t / C.
  • Lower phase margin to 25°: the recovery rings — which is how phase margin is estimated from transient waveforms.
  • Reduce gate-drive strength: pass current becomes a ramp and undershoot grows sharply. Low-IQ LDOs stepping from light to heavy load are most prone to this.

What to look for

  • Defaults of 5 V → 3.3 V, 300 mA in SOT-23: about 0.5 W dissipated and TJ above 150 °C — right at the edge of thermal shutdown.
  • Switch to DFN or QFN: the same dissipation rises only 30–40 °C. Heat often limits an LDO before its current rating does.
  • Lower VIN to 3.5 V: dissipation falls a lot, but you approach dropout; the right-hand plot shows dropout growing with temperature.

What to look for

  • Ripple current ΔIL = VOUT(1−D)/(L·f). Halve the inductance and ripple doubles; the peak may hit saturation.
  • Drop the load to very little: auto mode enters DCM and keeps efficiency up; forced PWM drives the inductor current negative — poor light-load efficiency but fixed ripple.
  • Set VIN 20 V, VOUT 0.6 V, 4 MHz: the on-time falls below tON,min and the converter cannot hold a fixed frequency.

What to look for

  • The default 10 µF / 0603 / 6.3 V at 3.3 V, 85 °C and −20% tolerance leaves only about 3 µF.
  • Keep 10 µF but go to 0805 or a 10 V rating: DC-bias loss improves dramatically. That is cheaper than adding a capacitor.
  • Switch the dielectric to Y5V: almost no capacitance left when hot. Never use it for power.

Parameters output-voltage trim · 20 000 units

CP test limit is 70% of spec; FT judges against spec. Trim codes range from −2N−1 to 2N−1−1.

What to look for

  • Trim range = 2N × LSB. If it is too small, parts at the tails cannot be trimmed back and the code distribution piles up at the limits.
  • With a large LSB the post-trim residual is uniform over ±LSB/2 and Cpk will not rise; a small LSB needs more bits to cover the range.
  • Press "Pre-compensate package shift": the final distribution recentres and Cpk clearly improves. That is the value of CP / FT correlation analysis.
