SiC and data-center power: from the grid to the GPU

AI data-center racks have gone from a dozen kW to well over a hundred, and the industry is discussing 1 MW racks. Ten times the power means ten times the loss unless every conversion stage gets more efficient. Wide-bandgap semiconductors — silicon carbide (SiC) and gallium nitride (GaN) — are what push power supplies (PSUs) past 97–98% efficiency and 100 W/in³. Using Infineon's CoolSiC and CoolGaN as examples, this page explains where SiC is used in the data center, why, and what to watch from design through to complaints.

About the product information　Product and reference-design details come from Infineon's public announcements and trade-press coverage (see Sources), compiled in October 2026. Specifications, part numbers and availability change; Infineon's website and datasheets are authoritative. This page does not represent Infineon.

Why wide bandgap

The wider a semiconductor's bandgap, the higher the critical electric field it withstands. For the same voltage rating the drift region can be thinner and more heavily doped, so on-resistance falls dramatically; smaller devices also have smaller parasitic capacitance and charge, so they switch faster.

Recall Rsp ∝ BV2.5 from Process · LDMOS: with a critical field nearly 10× higher, the theoretical Rsp at the same rating is two to three orders of magnitude lower. That is why SiC has largely displaced Si MOSFETs above 650 V and competes with IGBTs above 1200 V.

The data-center power chain

From grid to GPU there are roughly five to seven conversion stages, and total efficiency is the product:

ηtotal = ηUPS × ηPSU × ηIBC × ηVRM　e.g. 0.97 × 0.975 × 0.975 × 0.90 ≈ 0.83

The VRM stage (0.8 V, thousands of amps) is usually the least efficient and belongs to the world of multiphase bucks; SiC contributes mainly in the high-voltage AC/DC and UPS stages upstream. In a 100 MW data center, every percentage point of PSU efficiency removes about 1 MW of heat — and the cooling power that goes with it.

Rack PSUs: which stage uses SiC

A server PSU usually has two stages: power-factor correction (PFC) rectifies the AC and boosts it to about 400 V DC, then an isolated DC/DC (commonly an LLC resonant converter) steps down to a busbar of around 50 V.

  • Bridgeless totem-pole PFC: removes the bridge-rectifier diode drops for the highest efficiency, but its fast leg hard-switches in continuous conduction, where body-diode reverse recovery is very costly. Si super-junction Qrr is far too large; SiC and GaN are what made totem-pole PFC mass-producible.
  • Multi-level PFC: a three-level flying-capacitor topology puts only half the voltage on each switch, allowing 400 V-class devices and smaller inductors. This is the application Infineon's 400 V CoolSiC targets.
  • Isolated LLC DC/DC: soft-switched and able to run at several hundred kHz or more; GaN shrinks the transformer, though SiC or Si designs exist too.
  • Hold-up time: after AC loss the output must stay up for 10–20 ms, traditionally with large electrolytics that take a lot of space. Newer designs use active energy-buffer circuits to cut the capacitance needed.
  • Efficiency requirements: 80 PLUS Titanium, OCP Open Rack v3 and similar set high efficiency at both heavy and half load (on the order of 96% or more, depending on version and input voltage).

800 V HVDC and solid-state transformers

  • Why high-voltage DC: above 100 kW per rack, a 54 V busbar carries thousands of amps; copper weight, drop and space become the bottleneck. At 800 V the current for the same power falls to about 1/15.
  • Architecture change: AC-to-DC conversion moves from each server's PSU to a power rack or sidecar; inside the server an efficient 800 V-to-low-voltage stage is needed — a new battleground for SiC and GaN.
  • Solid-state transformers (SST): high-frequency SiC converters replace bulky 50/60 Hz transformers and go from medium-voltage grid straight to 800 V DC. Still emerging, with high-voltage SiC (1200–3300 V and beyond) at the core.
  • New safety issues: DC arcs do not self-extinguish, so solid-state circuit breakers (SSCB, another SiC candidate), insulation monitoring and new maintenance practices are needed.

SiC design points

TopicKey points
Gate-drive voltageSiC has lower transconductance and needs a higher turn-on voltage (commonly +15 to +20 V, part dependent) to reach rated RDS(on); turn off at 0 V or negative (−2 to −5 V) to prevent false turn-on. Follow the datasheet; do not reuse a Si 12 V drive.
Miller false turn-onHigh dV/dt from the other switch in a half bridge pulls the gate up through Cgd; counter with negative off-voltage, a Miller clamp and a low-impedance turn-off path.
Kelvin sourceWhen power and gate loops share the source pin, di/dt across source inductance fights the gate voltage and slows switching; four-pin packages (TO-247-4, TOLL, D²PAK-7) with a Kelvin source fix this.
Layout and parasiticsdV/dt can reach 50–100 V/ns: minimise power-loop inductance (see Applications · layout) and use isolated drivers with high CMTI so common-mode noise does not upset control.
Short-circuit protectionSiC dies are small with high current density, so short-circuit withstand is usually shorter than an IGBT's (around 2–3 µs). DESAT detection must be fast, with soft turn-off to limit overshoot.
Body diodeUsable with small Qrr, but high forward drop (about 3–4 V); keep dead time short or use synchronous rectification.
Thermal and RDS(on)SiC RDS(on) rises less with temperature than Si, but still rises; calculate losses at maximum junction temperature.

Manufacturing: why SiC is expensive

  • Substrates: SiC boules grow by sublimation (PVT) above 2000 °C — slowly and with low yield — so wafers cost far more than Si. The mainstream is moving from 150 mm to 200 mm to lower cost per die.
  • Crystal defects: micropipes, basal plane dislocations (BPD) and stacking faults cause failures or long-term degradation; the epitaxial layer must convert BPDs into harmless threading edge dislocations.
  • High-temperature processing: implant activation needs annealing above 1600 °C; the SiC/SiO2 interface has more defects than Si's, affecting channel mobility and VT stability.
  • Planar vs trench gate: trench gates give better channel mobility and Rsp, but the trench bottom field must be shielded; Infineon CoolSiC uses a trench structure.
  • Packaging: high temperature and current make silver-sinter die attach, copper wire / clips and diffusion-solder interconnects such as .XT increasingly common, for longer power-cycling life.

Compare Production · yield: for SiC, defect yield drives cost; larger dies yield worse, which is why high-current modules often parallel many small dies.

Test and reliability

ItemWhat is special about SiC
VT drift (BTI)Interface traps charge and discharge under gate bias, shifting VT; besides static HTGB, the industry runs dynamic gate-stress (AC switching) tests, and preconditioning and timing affect readings.
Gate-oxide lifetimeSiC interfaces and defects raise extrinsic early-failure risk; tightened gate-voltage screening and TDDB lifetime extrapolation are common.
Bipolar degradationUnder prolonged body-diode conduction, recombination energy grows BPDs into stacking faults, raising VF and RDS(on); buffer epi layers and body-diode stress tests screen it.
Cosmic rays (SEB)High-voltage devices at high field can be destroyed by atmospheric neutrons (single-event burnout); FIT rises steeply with applied voltage as a fraction of rating, so HVDC designs need voltage derating.
Avalanche and short circuitUnclamped inductive switching (UIS) measures avalanche energy; short-circuit withstand time sets driver-protection design.
Dynamic parametersDouble-pulse testing (DPT) measures Eon / Eoff and Qrr; it needs high-bandwidth current probes and a very low-inductance fixture.
Qualification standardsDiscretes are usually based on JEDEC / AEC-Q101 plus SiC-specific items; automotive power modules reference AQG 324. Data-center customers often have their own lifetime and FIT requirements.

The complaint flow and FA methods follow Customer complaints. SiC FA often uses thermal imaging and cross-sections to find burn sites in the gate oxide or trench bottom, and distinguishes EOS / short-circuit / SEB by the burn location and shape to identify the energy source.

Glossary

Wide bandgap (WBG)
Semiconductors with a bandgap clearly larger than silicon's, such as SiC and GaN.
Totem-pole PFC
Bridgeless totem-pole power-factor correction, a high-efficiency PFC topology without a rectifier bridge.
LLC
Inductor-inductor-capacitor resonant converter, common for soft-switched isolated DC/DC.
Hold-up time
How long the output stays in spec after input power is lost.
HVDC
High-voltage direct-current distribution.
SST
Solid-state transformer.
IBC
Intermediate bus converter (e.g. 48 V → 12 V).
BPD
Basal plane dislocation, the source of SiC bipolar degradation.
SEB
Single-event burnout caused by a particle strike.
CMTI
Common-mode transient immunity of an isolated driver.
DPT
Double-pulse test, the standard method for switching characterisation.
